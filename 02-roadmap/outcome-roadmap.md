## 2. Trade-off memo

> **Rock #1:** #4 Simplify the daily log, 14 fields to 4 (Impact 2.5, Effort 4): This is the core of the first release, turning the daily log from a form the field fills in into a record the superintendent approves.
>
> **Rock #2:** #7 + #1 Foreman app MVP with offline capture (Impact 2, Effort 10): These are merged into one Rock because both at full scope won’t fit. The MVP saves captures offline and uploads them when there’s signal, starting around month 3.
>
> **Rock #3:** #11 Schedule-change notifications through the messaging bridge (Impact 1.5, Effort 4): This is the cheapest field item left. It reaches crews who never install the app, and it also helps KR2.
>
> **Hard No #1:** #5 Compliance checklist generator: It puts mandatory office forms on a foreman’s phone, which is exactly why the field rejected Meridian before.
>
> **Hard No #2:** #12 Time-tracking module: Payroll entry mainly serves the office, so the field would get daily data entry and nothing they value.
>
> **Hard No #3:** #10 Executive KPI dashboard: It depends on the field reporting for the office’s benefit. The at-risk renewals belong to the enterprise roadmap, and the strategy keeps the Foundations team off that roadmap.

## Lab notes

**Show and swap**

- **Do the Rock selections feel traceable to a clear strategy, or do they read like a feature list?:** Yes, the Rocks are clearly connected to the strategy of improving field adoption. They focus on making the product easier for field workers and reducing manual work
- **Pick one Hard No and make the case for why it should actually be a Rock.:** I would challenge the Compliance Checklist decision. If compliance is required by enterprise customers, it could be important for adoption and revenue. I would validate the customer impact before deciding to delay it.

**Review and refine**

- **Does every Now item read as a strategic bet, or does it sound like a feature description?:** Partly bets. The If/then structure helps, but each “if” is still a feature: 4 fields, a foreman app, notifications through the bridge. A real bet names the belief behind the feature and the signal that would prove it wrong. Bet 2 is the weakest, because it reads as “build an app.”
- **Can you trace every Now item back to one of your OKRs?:** Each one has a label, but bet 3’s link is weak. KR2 measures the office asking superintendents for status. Sending schedule changes to crews moves information the other way, so it doesn’t reduce those requests. What actually reduces them is bet 1, because the office can read the auto-filled log instead of asking. KR3 also has no bet of its own.
- **If someone who had never seen your strategy read the Now column, would they know what problem you are solving this quarter?:** No. The Now column says what we’ll try, not why. It never says the field rejected Meridian as an office reporting tool, or that the office chases superintendents for status.
