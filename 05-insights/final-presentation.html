Meridian: The Field Gap

The case

Five deliverables, one strategy

Each lab committed one decision. Together they are the bet, the sequence, the team that owns it, the money, and what you learned building it.

Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Winning aspiration: A superintendent ends every day with nothing left to report, because what they captured on site already is the record, and the office never has to ask.

Where to play: We start with superintendents in 5 to 8 enterprise accounts, plus a pilot with 2 to 3 mid-market firms that already use Meridian's office platform but not in the field. Our first release does one thing well: turning on-site photos, voice notes, and texts into the daily log.

How to win: Only Meridian knows the project, so field captures file themselves and keep the schedule current as a byproduct of the work. Superintendents review and approve the daily log before it goes to the office, so the record reflects their judgment rather than an unfiltered photo dump.

The hard no

We will not ship any field feature whose main beneficiary is the office, because the field already rejected Meridian once for being a reporting tool, and every mandatory form on a foreman's phone sends them back to the group text.

Objective and key results

Make Meridian the tool superintendents choose to run their day, and prove the field will pay for it.

Increase the share of superintendents at sites without an app mandate who use Meridian on site at least 4 days a week, from under 10% to 50%.

Reduce office status requests to superintendents by 50%, against a baseline measured in month 1 through a two-week diary study.

Convert 2 of 3 mid-market pilot firms from a free field pilot to a paid field expansion.

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

Now: We bet that turning the photos, voice notes, and texts superintendents already send into the daily log will end end-of-day reporting and office status calls for superintendents in our design-partner accounts. (KR1, KR2)

Next: We bet that giving foremen their own app that captures without signal will make daily use as high at remote sites as at connected ones for foremen and superintendents. (KR1)

Later: We bet that pricing field capture per project instead of per seat will turn free field pilots into paid expansions for mid-market firms. (KR3)

Trade-off memo

#4 Simplify the daily log, 14 fields to 4 (Impact 2.5, Effort 4): This is the core of the first release, turning the daily log from a form the field fills in into a record the superintendent approves.

#7 + #1 Foreman app MVP with offline capture (Impact 2, Effort 10): These are merged into one Rock because both at full scope won’t fit. The MVP saves captures offline and uploads them when there’s signal, starting around month 3.

#11 Schedule-change notifications through the messaging bridge (Impact 1.5, Effort 4): This is the cheapest field item left. It reaches crews who never install the app, and it also helps KR2.

#5 Compliance checklist generator: It puts mandatory office forms on a foreman’s phone, which is exactly why the field rejected Meridian before.

#12 Time-tracking module: Payroll entry mainly serves the office, so the field would get daily data entry and nothing they value.

#10 Executive KPI dashboard: It depends on the field reporting for the office’s benefit. The at-risk renewals belong to the enterprise roadmap, and the strategy keeps the Foundations team off that roadmap.

Module 3
Team charter
What we own

What this team owns: Foundations owns turning on-site captures into the daily log superintendents approve: the messaging bridge, the 4-field daily log, the foreman app MVP with offline capture, and schedule-change notifications. PM 1 owns field adoption and mobile. PM 2 owns the design-partner accounts, the interface with sales, and the retention guardrail, defined as gross revenue retention at or above the month-1 baseline.

Out of scope: Renewal-driven features, the executive dashboard, the Procore fix and the web UI refresh belong to the platform and enterprise teams. A field-facing compliance form and manual time entry are Hard Nos that only the executive sponsor can reopen.

Joint calls across boundaries: Moving engineers off a Rock, office-serving field features, renewal promises, and changes to how data passes between the bridge, daily log and enterprise platform all require consulting both PMs. The product lead makes the final call on each, and the executive sponsor approves pricing and the expansion gates.

How we decide

Feature and scope calls: PM 1 decides field scope and sequencing within the Rocks, and the product lead has the final call on engineering allocation, field scope and Hard Nos. The engineering lead can veto commitments that exceed capacity, and the product lead then cuts scope or names what gets displaced. Sales cannot commit scope or dates before approval.

Escalations from outside the team: The executive sponsor decides at the next weekly leadership meeting, with the head of enterprise sales, plus the platform team lead when platform capacity is involved. If a renewal deadline comes sooner, the sponsor decides asynchronously within 2 business days.

Module 5
Financial model
The business case

Business Case:
Rock #4, Simplify the Daily Log (Stage 1)

Framed as a staged option: Fund a first stage to buy evidence, then decide. Stress-tested twice by an independent CFO review; approved in this form. All figures are assumptions until month-1 data replaces them.

The strategic bet: We spend about $1M over six months (Nov 2026 to Apr 2027) to learn whether a 4-field, capture-filled daily log makes superintendents choose Meridian on their own and cuts office status requests. If it does, that voluntary use protects enterprise renewals and makes a per-project mid-market add-on sellable, and only then do we fund the full return case.

The assumptions (ranked):
1) Voluntary field use lifts enterprise gross retention. It carries more than half the eventual return and can’t be measured on 5 to 8 accounts within six months, so Stage 1 tests it only through leading indicators.

2) Superintendents approve an auto-filled log daily without a mandate. Stage 1 tests this directly.

3) Captured field data cuts office status requests. Tested against the month-1 diary baseline.

4) The office accepts 4 fields as a sufficient record. Tested in design-partner accounts. Mid-market economics: about $300 per project per month, CAC about $20K per firm, 80% gross margin, 15% annual churn.

5) The pilot tests the price. 5 engineers ship the log by Fri Jan 29, 2027. The expected return Stage 1 (to Fri Apr 30, 2027): Evidence, not revenue. About $1M buys a fund-or-stop decision before the roughly $2M Stage 2 commitment.

6) Per customer, mid-market: A 10-project firm brings about $36K of revenue and $28.8K of gross profit a year. Payback is about 8 months once paid, or about 11 including the free pilot. LTV:CAC is 9.6:1, and 4.8:1 with churn 10 points higher and CAC 20% higher.

7) Per customer, enterprise: Each point of retention lift is worth about $10K a year per account, or $380K across all 38. At scale: Covering the team's $2M a year on gross margin needs about a $2.5M revenue run rate (for example, a 3-point lift of about $1.1M plus about 38 mid-market firms at about $1.4M). The full payback case, on margin and with CAC included, is written at the April gate using real baselines.

Kill criterion

Stage 1 gate, Fri Apr 30, 2027 (91 days after ship): Stage 2 is released only if at least 25% of superintendents at non-mandated design-partner sites approve and log-into Meridian at least 4 days a week, and office status requests to them are down at least 30% from the month-1 baseline.

If either misses, the roughly $2M Stage 2 budget (May 2027 to Apr 2028) is not released, 3 of the 5 engineers return to the platform team, and the paid mid-market add-on is not launched. Spend is capped at about $1M through April; the remaining team continues only under the messaging bridge's own budget.

Ship-slip rule: If the log hasn't shipped by Fri Feb 26, 2027, Stage 2 is not released, whatever the metrics say.

Pilot price test, pilot day 120: At least 2 of 3 pilot firms are paying $300 per project per month, or the mid-market add-on is not launched at that price.

Stage 2 retention checkpoint, Fri Oct 29, 2027: At least 60% of design-partner buyers (for example, 5 of 8) cite field use as a reason to renew in a documented renewal conversation, with no design-partner loss attributed to the field product.

If it misses, the roughly $1M left for Nov 2027 to Apr 2028 is withheld, Gate 1 does not start, and the case returns to the sponsor as a mid-market-only bet that must pay back on its own.

Module 6

What I learned

Where the strategy was hardest to write, what you will keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was getting my head around construction, a B2B world I didn’t know, and then making real trade-offs in it, especially once the reality check showed how little fit in 9 months. I worked through it by using AI as a mentor, asking questions as I went and learning even when I missed the mark, and by anchoring every cut to the Hard No and KR1.

Key learnings

I’ll keep looking for the data before deciding, using hard nos to protect focus, and thinking through each choice in its real context. Above all, I’ll push past the noise to find the best answer, not just the first one.

Aha moment

The stress-test prompts changed how I think about strategy: what started as cut-and-paste turned into digging into the scenario and finding that my original assumptions didn’t hold. We often move so fast that we rush decisions, and the better path is to slow down and let data, stress testing, and thoughtful product management win.
