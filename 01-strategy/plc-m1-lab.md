# Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test, Module 1 Lab

## Write your one-page strategy
- **Winning aspiration: define winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics. What does success look like for the specific person you serve?:** A superintendent ends every day with nothing left to report, because what they captured on site already is the record, and the office never has to ask.
- **Where to play: segment, geography, and use case, explicitly. Which customers, markets, and surfaces will you focus on?:** We start with superintendents in 5 to 8 enterprise accounts, plus a pilot with 2 to 3 mid-market firms that already use Meridian's office platform but not in the field. Our first release does one thing well: turning on-site photos, voice notes, and texts into the daily log.
- **How to win: your differentiator. What can you do that your specific competitors cannot easily replicate?:** Only Meridian knows the project, so field captures file themselves and keep the schedule current as a byproduct of the work. Superintendents review and approve the daily log before it goes to the office, so the record reflects their judgment rather than an unfiltered photo dump.
- **Capabilities required: what you must be world-class at. What will you build, buy, or partner for?:** We build contextual auto-tagging and ship the messaging bridge first, so the field gets value without installing anything, then add the mobile app once trust is earned. We buy speech, translation, and image recognition, and partner with messaging providers and design-partner customers.
- **Management systems: the metrics and rituals that keep your choices alive quarter to quarter.:** We fund Foundations in stages, judge it by voluntary use rather than mandated use, and report enterprise retention alongside mid-market pilot conversion. We protect the team of 10 from the enterprise roadmap and expand only when the field has chosen Meridian on its own.

## Name your one hard no
- **Your one hard no:** We will not ship any field feature whose main beneficiary is the office, because the field already rejected Meridian once for being a reporting tool, and every mandatory form on a foreman's phone sends them back to the group text.

## Write your three OKRs
- **Objective:** Make Meridian the tool superintendents choose to run their day, and prove the field will pay for it.
- **KR1:** Increase the share of superintendents at sites without an app mandate who use Meridian on site at least 4 days a week, from under 10% to 50%.
- **KR2:** Reduce office status requests to superintendents by 50%, against a baseline measured in month 1 through a two-week diary study.
- **KR3:** Convert 2 of 3 mid-market pilot firms from a free field pilot to a paid field expansion.

## AI pressure-test
- **Which challenge from the AI is most valid, and why?:** The most valid challenge was that KR1 measured use, not choice. A general contractor could hit “60% of daily logs created from captures” by mandating the app, which would make the metric look successful while the strategy failed. It exposed that my core thesis, that the field will choose Meridian on its own, wasn’t measured anywhere in my OKRs.
- **What would you change based on the pushback, and what would you defend?:** What would you change based on the pushback?

KR1: Now measures voluntary use at sites without an app mandate.
KR2: Baseline comes from a month-1 diary study instead of an assumption.
KR3: Replaced the evidence-retrieval KR with mid-market pilot conversion, to give the board a business signal.
Where to play: Added a parallel mid-market pilot, limited to firms already using Meridian’s office platform so our differentiator applies.
Sequencing: Ship the messaging bridge before the mobile app, so the field gets value without changing habits.
How to win: Superintendents approve the daily log, which addresses liability concerns while still creating a complete record.
Management systems: Added an enterprise retention guardrail and narrowed the first release to fit a team of five engineers.

What would you defend?

The hard no: It held up under both rounds of critique and is the filter that keeps us from rebuilding the reporting tool the field already rejected.
Enterprise accounts as the primary beachhead: That’s where Meridian’s project data exists, which is what makes our differentiator real.
The differentiator itself: The concern about outdated project data is valid, but the answer is that field captures keep the data current as a byproduct of the work, not as an extra task.
The ring-fenced team: Protecting the team of 10 from the enterprise roadmap is what makes any of this possible.
