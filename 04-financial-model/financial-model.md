## Lab notes

**Make your evaluation and funding decision**

- **What assumption is doing the most work? If this number is 20-30% off, what changes?:** The 6% to 8% upsell lift. Only 2 points are incremental: 400 × 2% = 8 upsells, not 32. That’s $224,000, not $896,000, and payback is about 9.6 months, not 2.4. Lift 20 to 30% short: 5.6 to 6.4 upsells, $157,000 to $179,000, payback 12 to 14 months. 8% rate 20 to 30% short: 5.6% to 6.4%, which is a lift of 0 to 0.4 points, $0 to $45,000, and payback of about 4 years or never. The 7% kill line fires only if the lift misses by more than 50%. A 30% miss lands at 7.4% and passes.
- **What is the structural problem in this case? Look past the headline numbers for something that does not hold up on closer inspection.:** The 20% faster quote is delivered in Standard, but the return requires accounts to leave Standard. The bet names no Enterprise-only capability to explain the move from 6% to 8%. The kill criterion measures upsell, not the 20%, so a feature that works but keeps accounts in Standard would read as failure. The $620 Standard CAC has no role in upsell math; it only matters if the real value is retention.
- **Is the kill criterion complete and actionable? Does it name the consequence, or hand the decision back to the room?:** It names a consequence but leaves the room to define it: “Paused” is undefined for accounts that already have the feature. “Reallocated” names no destination and no owner. “End of Q3” isn’t tied to launch or to the two-quarter ramp. There’s no rule between 7% and 8% (28 to 31 of 400 accounts). It protects only Q4 capacity, not the $180,000 build.
- **Your verdict: FUND / FUND WITH ONE CONDITION / DO NOT FUND. If a condition, name it; otherwise explain in one sentence.:** FUND WITH ONE CONDITION: name the Enterprise-only capability before the $180,000 build. Without it, the 2-point lift worth $224,000 has no mechanism.
